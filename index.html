CHAPTER ONE

A New School

Senior Five was supposed to be a new beginning.

New subjects. New classmates. New teachers. New routines.

And, for me, a new school.

I had joined Gombe Secondary School in Senior Five, entering the BCM class known as S5 C2. Everything still felt unfamiliar. I was learning the environment, learning the people around me, and slowly finding my place among students who would become part of my everyday life.

I didn't know that somewhere among those unfamiliar faces was someone I had already seen before.

Not in reality.

In a dream.

It had happened perhaps one or two years earlier. I had dreamt about a girl sending me a message through mail to her school, Kibuli Secondary School.

At the time, it was just a dream.

I didn't know her.

I didn't know where life would take me.

I certainly didn't imagine that one day I would walk into a new school and meet someone who somehow reminded me of that dream.

Then I met Habiba.

02

CHAPTER TWO

The Girl Behind Me

Habiba was a quiet girl.

At least, that was my first impression of her.

She seemed shy, almost reserved. She wasn't the kind of person who immediately drew attention by trying to be the loudest person in the room.

Our classroom was arranged in groups.

My group and hers were positioned opposite each other, with a narrow row between them where students could walk toward the back or move toward the front of the classroom.

Two groups. One narrow row. And two people who had not yet realized how memorable those seats would become.

She often sat behind me.

Sometimes, while sitting in our respective groups, we would playfully tap or hit each other on the back.

Nothing serious.

Just those little moments that happen between classmates.

At first, I didn't think much of it.

She was simply another person in my new class.

But eventually, I got the opportunity to actually talk to her.

And that was when everything became a little different.

03

CHAPTER THREE

Fifteen Minutes

I wasn't particularly talkative in class.

I could be quiet and somewhat nonchalant, keeping to myself and observing what was happening around me.

Then one day, another girl called me.

She seemed to be teasing me, trying to get my attention.

Somehow, she succeeded.

And that small moment became the beginning of something I would remember much longer than I expected.

Habiba spoke to me.

It wasn't some extraordinary conversation.

There was no dramatic moment.

No great declaration.

Just two students talking.

But I noticed something.

Her words had a certain sweetness to them.

I found myself enjoying the conversation more than I expected.

For the first time, the quiet girl who had been sitting behind me wasn't simply someone I recognized in class.

She became someone I wanted to know.

So over the following days, I tried talking to her again.

Nothing too serious.

Small conversations.

Short exchanges.

Little moments between lessons and school activities.

Sometimes we would talk only briefly before returning to whatever we were doing.

But somehow, those brief conversations mattered to me.

04

CHAPTER FOUR

The Conversation I Kept

Time moved quickly.

Lessons became routines.

Days became weeks.

Weeks became months.

And before I realized it, the end of first term was approaching.

Yet I still hadn't learned very much about Habiba.

I didn't know her story.

I didn't know everything she liked.

I didn't know what she thought about when she was alone.

I didn't know the things that made her laugh or the things she cared about most.

I simply knew the girl I had encountered in S5 C2.

The shy girl.

The girl who sat behind me.

The girl whose words I had somehow found unusually sweet.

And then there was that one conversation.

Perhaps fifteen minutes.

Fifteen minutes isn't a long time.

In a normal day, fifteen minutes can disappear without anyone noticing.

But that conversation stayed with me.

When first term finally ended and everyone began preparing for the holiday, I carried that memory with me.

I thought about it again and again.

It became one of those small moments that somehow feels bigger after it has passed.

I didn't know why it mattered so much.

Maybe it was because I had only just met her.

Maybe it was because I still knew so little about her.

Or maybe it was because somewhere in the back of my mind was that strange old dream from one or two years earlier.

A dream about a girl. A girl connected somehow to a school. And now, unexpectedly, I had met someone who made that forgotten dream feel almost familiar.
✦

I went into the holiday carrying very little information about Habiba.

But I carried a memory.

Fifteen minutes.

A few smiles.

A few words.

A quiet girl from S5 C2.

And somehow, that was enough to make her memorable.

I didn't know what the next term would bring.

I didn't know whether we would become closer friends.

I didn't know what conversations were waiting for us.

I didn't even know whether she thought about those moments the way I did.

All I knew was that first term had ended.

And somewhere between the beginning of Senior Five and the end of that first term, a girl named Habiba had quietly become part of my story.

And the story was only beginning.
